# Blockchain for Verified Emissions at DHL

Report for Digital Economy, Tools and Techniques (Assessment 1), MSc Business Analytics, University of Greenwich, 2025–26. The company was chosen as a group; the report was researched and written individually.

## Question

DHL's emissions and carbon-offset data comes from carriers, subcontractors, facilities and external verifiers, each using different methods, systems and timelines. How can DHL make that data accurate, traceable and verifiable across the whole network?

## Approach

1. Identified the core issue as verification rather than data volume: no shared mechanism establishes trust between many independent parties, and carbon-offset markets have well-documented credibility problems.
2. Assessed what a blockchain adds: a shared ledger that all authorised partners see in the same form, tamper-resistant audit trails, no single party controlling the data, more credible carbon claims, and faster cross-border compliance checks.
3. Weighed the barriers: throughput limits at enterprise scale, uneven digital maturity among partners, the lack of common standards and governance, and the risk of exposing commercially sensitive data.

## Recommendations

- Adopt a permissioned blockchain as a verification layer that works alongside DHL's existing ERP and sustainability systems at first, starting with a small pilot on selected routes, with the eventual aim of removing reliance on legacy systems.
- Use smart contracts to check emissions and offset data against agreed criteria before it is recorded.
- Protect privacy with role-based access, selective disclosure and field-level encryption, while keeping records auditable.
- Run the network through a neutral consortium of transport partners, auditors and sustainability specialists, so it is shared infrastructure rather than a DHL-controlled platform.
- Offer tiered participation so less digitally mature partners can still contribute verified data.
- Implement in phases, measured on audit efficiency, data accuracy and reporting transparency.

## Limitations

- The benefits depend on wide partner participation, which DHL cannot guarantee.
- The report gives no cost estimate or detailed timeline for the phases.
- The report draws on published research and DHL's own reports rather than internal DHL data.
