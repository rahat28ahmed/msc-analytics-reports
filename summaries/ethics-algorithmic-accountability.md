# Ethics and Accountability in Algorithmic Decision-Making

Essay for Ethical and Legal Aspects of Business Analytics, MSc Business Analytics, University of Greenwich, 2025–26.

## Question

When an algorithm makes or shapes a decision about a person, who is accountable for the outcome, and what do ethics and law require of the organisation that uses it?

## Approach

The essay defines transparency as how clearly an algorithm's decision process can be understood, and accountability as who is responsible for its outcomes. It applies deontology and virtue ethics to COMPAS and Clearview AI, and compares EU, UK and US law through the Uber and Clearview cases.

- COMPAS (criminal justice risk scores): Northpointe refused to disclose how the tool worked, citing intellectual property, although it was shown to label Black defendants as high risk more often than white defendants with similar records. Judged under deontology, keeping the model secret fails the duty to treat defendants as ends in themselves; under virtue ethics it shows a failure of honesty and fairness.
- The 2018 Uber self-driving fatality in Arizona: used to examine who is responsible when an autonomous system causes harm: the operator, the manufacturer or the developer. The essay compares the EU's binding approach (GDPR Articles 5 and 22, the AI Act's treatment of autonomous vehicles as high-risk, and strict product liability) with the US reliance on voluntary guidance such as NHTSA's Automated Driving Systems framework and Automated Vehicles 4.0.
- Clearview AI (facial recognition): traced from the ICO's £7.5 million fine in May 2022 to the UK Upper Tribunal ruling in October 2025, which confirmed that UK data protection law applies to a foreign company monitoring UK residents.

## Conclusions and Recommendations

- Organisations should treat transparency as a moral duty as well as a legal one.
- Companies should publish transparency reports covering data sources, bias tests and compliance measures, and set up internal ethics review boards.
- Privacy-preserving methods such as federated learning and differential privacy can protect personal data while keeping systems useful.
- Cross-border enforcement is needed, because uneven regulation lets a company face strict accountability in one region and little in another.

## Limitations

- The analysis rests on three high-profile cases, which may not represent everyday algorithmic decisions in business.
- The legal comparison covers the EU, the UK and the US only.
